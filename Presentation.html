Section 1 of 10 · Background

First, What is ABM?

Let's start with the basics. If you've never heard of Account-Based Marketing, this section is for you.

📚 Plain English Explanation

Account-Based Marketing (ABM) is a B2B (business-to-business) sales strategy where instead of marketing to everyone, companies focus on a small list of specific high-value target companies ("accounts") and create personalized campaigns just for them.

Example: A software company that sells to enterprises won't advertise to random people. Instead, they'll identify "50 banks with over 10,000 employees that use Salesforce" — and then craft custom outreach for each one. That's ABM.

💡

The Core Concept

ABM follows a simple but powerful workflow that traditionally requires weeks of manual work:

  • Step 1: Identify ideal customers Define what your perfect customer looks like (industry, size, tech they use, location, etc.) — this is called an "Ideal Customer Profile" or ICP.
  • Step 2: Find matching companies Research databases to find real companies that match the ICP. Usually takes hours per company.
  • Step 3: Research decision-makers Find out who at each company makes buying decisions, what they care about, what pain points they have.
  • Step 4: Personalize outreach Write custom emails, LinkedIn messages, or scripts tailored to each company and person.
  • Step 5: Multi-channel engagement Reach out via email, LinkedIn, phone, SMS, ads — often multiple touchpoints over weeks.
  • Step 6: Track and qualify responses Monitor who responds, classify if they're interested, and hand them off to sales when ready.
Section 2 of 10 · The Problem

Why ABM is Broken Today

Doing ABM manually is slow, expensive, and inconsistent. Here's why most companies struggle with it.

🤔 The Reality Check

A typical B2B sales team spends 60–80% of their time on prospecting — researching companies, finding emails, writing messages — rather than actually selling. Most outreach gets ignored because it's generic. Companies waste millions on tools that don't talk to each other. Our project solves all of this.

⚠️

The Pain Points

  • Manual research is exhausting Sales reps spend 70% of their time researching companies one-by-one instead of selling. A single prospect takes 30+ minutes to research properly.
  • Generic outreach gets ignored Mass-blasted emails have less than 2% reply rates. Buyers can spot a template from a mile away and immediately delete it.
  • Tools don't talk to each other Marketing teams juggle 10+ disconnected platforms — CRM, email, LinkedIn, SMS, analytics — each in its own silo with no coordination.
  • No intelligent qualification Companies waste time chasing leads that will never buy because they have no way to score account fit objectively.
  • Personalization doesn't scale Truly personalized messages work, but writing them for 500 companies is humanly impossible.
  • No learning loop Teams repeat the same mistakes campaign after campaign because there's no feedback mechanism between marketing, sales, and clients.
✨

How ABM Engine Solves It

  • AI agents handle the research 10+ specialized AI agents pull data from Apollo, Crunchbase, and BuiltWith automatically, saving 95% of research time.
  • LLM-powered personalization at scale Claude and GPT-4o-mini craft unique messages for every account using rich enrichment data — like having 100 expert copywriters.
  • One unified platform Email (Instantly), LinkedIn (Phantombuster), and SMS (Twilio) all orchestrated through a single dashboard with shared analytics.
  • 6-dimensional scoring Every account is scored on industry fit, company size, tech stack, geography, funding, and buying signals — ranked by likelihood to buy.
  • Human checkpoints (CP1–CP4) Four strategic review gates ensure humans approve critical decisions, preventing AI from making costly mistakes.
  • Continuous learning loop Client feedback automatically refines templates and prompts. The system gets smarter with every campaign.

Section 4 of 10 · How It Works

A Real-World Walkthrough

Let's walk through exactly what happens when a user opens our platform. We'll use a concrete example throughout.

📖 Our Example Scenario

Throughout this walkthrough, imagine you're "TechCorp" — a startup selling cybersecurity software. You want to find and engage 50 banks in the United States with over 5,000 employees that use AWS. Here's how our platform makes that happen, automatically.

1

🔐 User Logs In & Sets Up

The user (TechCorp's marketing team) opens our React-based web app at localhost:5173. They land on the Intake page — a clean form built with Tailwind CSS.

What happens behind the scenes:

React mounts the Intake component → fetches user session via Axios → backend (FastAPI) validates session → displays form.

React 18 TypeScript Tailwind FastAPI
2

📝 User Fills in Their ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

TechCorp enters their criteria into the form: industry = "Banking", company size = "5000+ employees", tech stack = "AWS", geography = "United States", budget = "$50K+".

Auto-save magic:

Every few seconds, the form data is saved to Redis with a 7-day expiration. If the user closes their browser, they can resume right where they left off.

Redis JSON Schema Pydantic
3

🔍 ICP Scout Agent Discovers Companies

When the user clicks "Discover", our ICP Scout Agent springs into action. It queries 4 external APIs in parallel to find matching companies.

Data sources hit:

Apollo.io (company database) → Harmonic.ai (startup funding) → Crunchbase (financial data) → BuiltWith (tech stack detection). Results are merged, deduplicated, and normalized.

Apollo.io Crunchbase BuiltWith Async Python
4

⚖️ AI Scores Each Account Across 6 Dimensions

For every discovered company (say, JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, Bank of America...), the system calculates a score from 0–100 based on how well they match.

Example score for JP Morgan:

Industry: 95/100 (banking ✓) · Size: 100/100 (250K employees ✓) · Tech: 90/100 (uses AWS ✓) · Geo: 100/100 (US-based ✓) · Funding: 100/100 (public) · Triggers: 75/100 (recent CISO hire) → Total: 93/100

Scoring Algorithm Pandas NumPy
5

🧠 Buyer Intelligence Enrichment

For top-scoring accounts, the Buyer Intel Agent goes deeper — it finds decision-makers, maps the org chart, identifies pain points, and researches procurement processes.

For JP Morgan, the system finds:

"CISO: Jane Smith (joined 6 months ago) · CFO: John Doe · Recent news: $50M cybersecurity budget approved · Pain points: legacy systems integration · Buying authority: requires VP+ sign-off"

LinkedIn Data News APIs LLM Analysis
6

✅ Checkpoint 2: Human Reviews & Approves

This is critical — before AI writes any messages, a human reviews the account list. They can edit scores, remove bad fits, or add notes. The system blocks progression until approval.

Why this matters:

Even the best AI makes mistakes. A human checkpoint prevents sending emails to wrong companies or based on incorrect data. This builds trust in AI systems.

CP-2 Agent Audit Logs RBAC
7

✍️ Storyteller Agent Writes Personalized Messages

For each approved account, the Storyteller Agent calls Claude (Anthropic) or GPT-4o-mini (OpenAI) with rich context to generate personalized outreach.

Generated email for JP Morgan:

"Hi Jane, Congrats on your recent move to JP Morgan! Given the $50M cybersecurity initiative announced last quarter, I thought you'd be interested in how TechCorp helped Goldman Sachs reduce their AWS attack surface by 60%..."

Claude API GPT-4o-mini Prompt Templates
8

💬 Checkpoint 3: Operator + Client Review Messages

Before sending, both the operator (marketing team) and end client (TechCorp's CEO) review and approve messages. Feedback flows back to refine templates.

The feedback loop:

If the CEO says "make it more casual" → the system updates the prompt template → all future messages improve. The system literally learns from feedback.

CP-3 Agent Template Versioning Feedback Loop
9

🚀 Campaign Agent Launches Multi-Channel Outreach

Approved messages are sent across 3 channels simultaneously. The Campaign Agent orchestrates everything with built-in safety features.

Multi-channel execution:

📧 Instantly.ai sends personalized emails · 💼 Phantombuster sends LinkedIn connection requests + DMs · 📱 Twilio sends follow-up SMS · All tracked in one dashboard with quota limits (max 50/day) and circuit breakers (stop on errors).

Instantly.ai Phantombuster Twilio Rate Limiting
10

📊 Real-Time Engagement Tracking

As prospects open emails, click links, reply, or bounce, our system captures every event via webhooks and updates the dashboard in real-time.

What gets tracked:

Email opens (47%), link clicks (12%), replies (8%), bounces (3%), LinkedIn acceptance (35%), SMS responses (15%). Engagement scores update live. Hot accounts flagged automatically.

🎯 Verifier Agent Classifies Responses & Hands Off to Sales

When prospects reply, the Verifier Agent uses LLMs to classify the response (positive, negative, neutral, out-of-office) and generates handoff notes for the sales team.

Example handoff note:

"🟢 HOT LEAD: Jane Smith (JP Morgan) replied positively. Mentioned Q4 budget. Suggested call next Tuesday. Suggested talking points: AWS security, compliance, Goldman case study. Lead score: 92/100."

Section 5 of 10 · The 9 Phases

Detailed Phase Breakdown

Each phase is a distinct module with its own agents, data, and UI. Here's exactly what each phase does and why it exists.

📝
Phase 1

Intake — Capturing the ICP

🎯 Purpose

The very first step. Users tell the system who their ideal customer is. This information drives every subsequent phase.

⚙️ What It Does
  • Presents a structured form with validated fields
  • Captures: industry, company size, budget, tech stack, geography
  • Validates data against JSON schemas (MasterContext)
  • Auto-saves drafts to Redis (7-day TTL)
  • Allows users to resume incomplete forms
Real Example

"TechCorp wants banks (SIC: 6021), 5000+ employees, $50K+ budget, using AWS, located in US/Canada/UK"

🔍
Phase 2

ICP Scout — Finding Real Companies

🎯 Purpose

Take the abstract ICP and find actual companies in the real world that match it.

⚙️ What It Does
  • Queries Apollo.io for company directories
  • Queries Harmonic.ai for funded startups
  • Queries Crunchbase for financial data
  • Queries BuiltWith for tech stack detection
  • Deduplicates and normalizes results
  • Scores each account across 6 dimensions
  • Supports CSV upload of existing prospect lists
Real Example

Returns: "JP Morgan Chase (score: 93), Goldman Sachs (91), Bank of America (88), Wells Fargo (85)..." — 50 ranked accounts total

🧠
Phase 3

Buyer Intelligence — Going Deep

🎯 Purpose

Discovery tells us which companies to target. Intelligence tells us who within those companies to talk to and what to say.

⚙️ What It Does
  • Identifies decision-makers (CISO, CFO, etc.)
  • Maps reporting relationships (org charts)
  • Researches recent news (funding, hires, layoffs)
  • Identifies pain points from industry signals
  • Analyzes procurement processes
  • Builds buyer personas using LLM synthesis
Real Example

"Target: Jane Smith, CISO at JP Morgan. Joined 6 months ago. Reports to CTO. Pain points: legacy AWS sprawl. Recent news: $50M security budget. Buying motion: VP+ sign-off required."

✅
Checkpoint 2

Human Review Gate — Account Approval

🎯 Purpose

The first hard stop in the workflow. A human must approve the account list and intelligence before AI writes any messages.

⚙️ What It Does
  • Presents reviewable dashboard with all accounts
  • Allows score editing, removal, additions
  • Captures rationale for changes (audit trail)
  • Enforces gate — blocks Phase 5 until approval
  • Logs all decisions for compliance
Why This Exists

AI can hallucinate. A human catching mistakes here (e.g., wrong industry classification) prevents 50 bad emails from being sent. It's the safety net that makes AI deployment trustworthy.

✍️
Phase 5 / CP-3

Storyteller + Messaging Review

🎯 Purpose

Generate personalized outreach for every account using LLMs, then have operators and clients review before sending.

⚙️ What It Does
  • Loads versioned prompt templates from DB
  • Injects buyer intel context into prompts
  • Calls Claude API (primary) or GPT-4o-mini (fallback)
  • Generates multi-tier outputs (tier-1/2/3)
  • Validates output against schema
  • Presents in operator review UI
  • Captures client feedback for refinement
Multi-Tier Concept

Tier-1: Highly personalized (used when rich data available). Tier-2: Moderate personalization. Tier-3: Generic fallback (used when data is sparse). This ensures every account gets something, even with imperfect data.

🚀
Phase 6

Campaign Execution — Outbound

🎯 Purpose

Actually send the messages across multiple channels and track every interaction.

⚙️ What It Does
  • Email sequences via Instantly.ai
  • LinkedIn automation via Phantombuster
  • SMS campaigns via Twilio
  • Enforces daily quotas (e.g., max 50 emails/day)
  • Circuit breakers stop on errors
  • Tracks opens, clicks, replies via webhooks
  • Updates engagement scores in real-time
Safety Features

Quota tracking prevents spam-flag penalties. Circuit breakers automatically pause campaigns if bounce rates spike. Rate limiting respects API provider limits. All critical for production deployment.

📊
Checkpoint 4

Campaign Performance Review

🎯 Purpose

Before handing leads to the sales team, operators review campaign performance and quality.

⚙️ What It Does
  • Aggregates campaign metrics
  • Shows engagement breakdowns by channel
  • Identifies hot accounts (high engagement)
  • Shows reply classification distribution
  • Approves which accounts to hand off
  • Final QA gate before sales
Example Dashboard

"Total sent: 150 · Opens: 71 (47%) · Clicks: 18 (12%) · Replies: 12 (8%) · Hot accounts: 8 · Approve for handoff?"

👥
Phase 8

Client Approval Portal

🎯 Purpose

A separate portal where the end client (TechCorp's CEO) can review qualified leads and provide final approval before sales contacts them.

⚙️ What It Does
  • Token-based access for clients
  • Shows curated list of qualified accounts
  • Displays engagement evidence
  • Captures approve/reject decisions
  • Collects feedback for continuous learning
  • Multi-stakeholder access support
Why This Exists

The client owns their brand. Letting them see and approve outreach before sales calls happen builds trust and prevents brand mishaps. The feedback also makes our AI smarter.

🎯
Phase 9

Verification & Sales Handoff

🎯 Purpose

The final phase — classify all responses, prepare clean handoff packages, and deliver them to the sales team.

⚙️ What It Does
  • Classifies replies via LLM (positive/negative/OOO/neutral)
  • Computes final lead score
  • Generates suggested talking points
  • Creates structured handoff notes
  • Pushes data to CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot, etc.)
  • Notifies sales team via Slack/Email
Handoff Note Example

"🟢 Jane Smith (JP Morgan, CISO) — Positive reply, mentioned Q4 budget, asked for case study, suggested call Tuesday 2PM. Lead score: 92/100. Suggested talking points: AWS security, compliance frameworks, Goldman Sachs case study."

Section 6 of 10 · Human-in-the-Loop

4 Strategic Checkpoints

AI is powerful but imperfect. These 4 checkpoints ensure humans remain in control of critical decisions.

🤝 Why Checkpoints Matter

Fully autonomous AI systems are dangerous in marketing — wrong messages can damage brand reputation, violate regulations, or waste budgets. Our 4 checkpoints (CP-1 to CP-4) ensure that at every critical decision point, a human reviews and approves. This is called "human-in-the-loop" architecture and it's considered best practice for production AI systems.

CP-1
📝

Intake Validation

Validates the ICP criteria before discovery starts

Prevents: Bad input data cascading into bad discovery results. "Garbage in, garbage out."
CP-2
✅

Account Approval

Human reviews discovered accounts and buyer intelligence

Prevents: Sending outreach to wrong companies, AI hallucinations, or unwanted prospects.
CP-3
💬

Message Review

Operator + client approve all LLM-generated messaging

Prevents: Brand damage from poor wording, factual errors, or off-brand messaging.
CP-4
📊

Campaign Approval

Performance review before sales handoff

Prevents: Low-quality leads wasting sales team time, ensures only qualified prospects proceed.

Section 10 of 10 · Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions

Click any question to expand the answer.

What does "agentic" mean in "Agentic ABM"?

"Agentic" means the system uses autonomous AI agents — small, specialized AI programs that each do one job well and pass results to the next agent. Instead of one big AI doing everything, we have a coordinated team of agents working together. This is the modern way to build production AI systems.

Why use both Claude AND GPT-4o-mini? Isn't one enough?

We use both for reliability and quality. Claude is typically better at nuanced, creative writing — perfect for personalized outreach. GPT-4o-mini is faster and cheaper, ideal as a fallback if Claude is rate-limited or down. Dual-LLM support means our system stays online even if one provider has an outage.

What happens if the AI generates a bad message?

That's exactly what Checkpoint 3 (CP-3) is for. Before any message is sent, both an operator and the client review and approve it. Bad outputs get rejected, edited, and the feedback refines the prompt template. The system literally learns from mistakes.

How does the system avoid getting users flagged as spam?

Multiple safeguards:

  • Daily quotas — limit sends per day (e.g., max 50 emails)
  • Circuit breakers — auto-pause if bounce rate spikes
  • Personalization — each message is unique, not bulk-sent
  • Multi-channel pacing — spreads engagement across email/LinkedIn/SMS
  • Rate limiting — respects API provider limits
What if I don't have API keys for Apollo, Crunchbase, etc.?

The system gracefully degrades. Each API key is optional — if you don't have one, that data source is skipped. We also support CSV upload for existing prospect lists, and a Mock transport for the campaign agent so you can test the full workflow without spending money on APIs.

Why FastAPI instead of Django or Flask?

FastAPI offers three killer features for our use case:

  • Native async support — critical for calling multiple AI APIs in parallel
  • Auto-generated Swagger docs — every endpoint documented automatically
  • Pydantic integration — type-safe request/response validation built-in

It's the fastest-growing Python web framework for good reason.

Why React + Vite instead of Next.js?

Our use case is a client-side dashboard application, not a content-heavy site needing SSR. Vite gives us instant hot-reload development experience and lightning-fast builds. Next.js would be overkill and add complexity we don't need.

How do I run this project locally?

Three terminal commands:

  • Backend: cd backend && uvicorn backend.main:app --reload
  • Frontend: cd frontend && npm run dev
  • Redis (optional): docker-compose up -d

Then visit localhost:5173 for the UI and localhost:8000/docs for API documentation.

Is this production-ready? Can a real company use it today?

The core architecture is production-grade — type-safe code, comprehensive tests, human checkpoints, circuit breakers, audit logs. However, real production deployment would need: SSL certificates, authentication (currently demo-level), monitoring/alerting (Datadog, etc.), and compliance reviews (GDPR, CAN-SPAM). The foundation is solid; remaining work is operational, not architectural.

What was the hardest part of building this?

The hardest challenge was balancing AI autonomy with human control. Pure AI automation is fast but risky; pure manual is safe but slow. Designing the 4-checkpoint architecture took multiple iterations to find the right balance — enough AI to save time, enough human oversight to prevent disasters. Getting the agent communication patterns right (clean inputs/outputs, error handling, retries) was also non-trivial.
